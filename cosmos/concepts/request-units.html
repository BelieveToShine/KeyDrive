📖 Why did this need to exist?

Page 2 spread the data and the load over partitions. But “load” needs a unit. In SQL you reasoned in logical reads and CPU milliseconds; in Cosmos DB the one number the service itself gives you with every response is the RU charge.

That makes cost visible per request, and it makes design decisions measurable: a document design, an index or a query shape that halves the RU charge halves a share of your bill and your throttling.

This page covers what an RU is, what raises or lowers it, and the three ways to buy RU/s.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

Stable across hardware. RUs are a normalised measure — the same request costs the same RU regardless of machine details; you budget in RU/s.

Autoscale details. Throughput scales between 0.1 × Tmax and Tmax; the minimum Tmax for a container is 1,000 RU/s; larger databases with many containers need a higher minimum. Billing follows the highest level reached in each hour.

Write cost. A 1 KB write costs roughly 5 RU (sources quote about 4.95–5.5 depending on indexing); more regions raise write RU consumption.

Cross-partition minimum. A query that checks a physical partition’s index is charged at least about 2.5 RU per partition, even if nothing matches (page 6).

Other engines. DynamoDB’s read/write capacity units play the same role; SQL’s nearest cousin is logical reads (SQL lab page 2) — a work measure, not a bill.

🔥 Interview recall
  • One RU ≈ the cost of a point read of a 1 KB item; the RU charge comes back with every response.
  • Writes cost more than reads; replace ≈ 2× insert; more indexed paths and bigger items cost more.
  • Strong and bounded-staleness reads cost about 2× the relaxed levels.
  • Over the RU/s budget → 429; the SDK retries.
  • Provisioned (steady), autoscale (10%–100% of Tmax, ≈ 1.5× rate), serverless (pay per request, single region).
  • Tuning = lower the RU charge of your most frequent operations.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"Cosmos DB charges in request units, a normalised bundle of CPU, memory and I/O, and returns the RU charge with every response. Point reads are cheapest, writes cost several times more and scale with item size and indexed paths, and queries depend on how much index and how many partitions they touch. I choose provisioned throughput for steady traffic, autoscale for spiky traffic and serverless for light workloads, and I tune by lowering the RU charge of my most frequent operations."
⚠️ Common trap

Provisioning for the peak “to be safe”. You pay for idle RUs all day. Autoscale exists for this.

Ignoring RequestCharge. If you do not log it, you cannot tell which endpoint is burning the budget.
