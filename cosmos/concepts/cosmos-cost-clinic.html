▶️ Watch it happen: how every case is solved
📖 Why did this need to exist?

In real life the ticket says “Cosmos is expensive” or “we get 429s”, not “you have a fan-out”. The cases start the way tickets do. All of them use the same orders container and the same illustrative numbers as the rest of the lab.

Every case is solvable with the ladder: measure the RU charge, read the diagnostics, find the biggest wrong thing, one hypothesis, one change, prove it.

🧭 The ladder, Cosmos edition
1 · MEASURE ★

📏 What did it cost?

The RU charge and latency of one request — a number, not a feeling.

2 · READ ★

🔬 Read the diagnostics

Query metrics, partitions touched, indexed vs retrieved documents.

3 · FIND ★

🎯 The biggest wrong thing

A fan-out, a scan, a hot key, a fat document?

4 · GUESS ★

💡 One hypothesis

Wrong partition key? Missing composite index? Wrong shape?

5 · CHANGE ★

🔧 Change one thing

Never two at once.

6 · PROVE ★

✅ Re-measure

Same request, same data, lower RU charge.

Ask in this order: 429s or just cost? → one partition or all? → retrieved ≈ output? → how many partitions touched? → how big is the item and how many paths are indexed? → which consistency level and how many regions?

🩺 Case 1

Throttled at night, quiet by day

Symptom. Writes to orders get 429s every evening; account-wide RU/s looks comfortable. The key is /orderDate.

Evidence.

  • Insights: one partition range at 100%, the others 20–35%.
  • Key-RU log: one value (“today”) takes ≈ 90% of write RU.
  • 429s ≈ 12% of inserts; reads fine.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. A hot key: every new order shares today’s date, so one partition takes all the writes.

Fix. A key that spreads writes — new container keyed by /customerId (the key cannot change in place), migrate, switch. Raising RU/s only masks it.

📖 Where to review: pages 2 and 8.

🩺 Case 2

The dashboard that costs 150 RU

Symptom. The “all open orders” dashboard refreshes constantly and costs about 150 RU per run; the bill is dominated by it.

Evidence.

  • Query metrics: partitions queried ≈ 50; retrieved ≈ output.
  • No partition key in the filter (status = "Open").

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Fan-out: ≈ 2.5 RU minimum per physical partition before any work.

Fix. A read model kept by the change feed (keyed so the dashboard hits one partition), or a precomputed summary item read with a point read — ≈ 1–10 RU.

📖 Where to review: pages 6 and 10.

🩺 Case 3

Writes cost 12 RU for a 2 KB order

Symptom. Inserts cost far more than expected; nobody filters on the long notes field or the lines array.

Evidence.

  • Indexing policy: default — every path indexed.
  • A 2 KB order ≈ 12 RU; the team expects ≈ 7.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Every indexed path is write work.

Fix. Include the root, exclude /notes/? and /lines/*; re-measure; check no query filters on them.

📖 Where to review: pages 3 and 5.

🩺 Case 4

Customer’s newest orders: 312 RU

Symptom. “Customer 42’s newest orders” is slow and expensive although the partition key is in the filter.

Evidence.

  • Metrics: retrieved 184,000, output 12, index utilisation 0%.
  • Query: WHERE customerId = 42 ORDER BY orderDate DESC.
  • One partition queried.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Not a fan-out; an index gap — the sort needs a composite index.

Fix. Composite (customerId asc, orderDate desc); wait for it to finish building; re-run → a few RU.

📖 Where to review: pages 5 and 7.

🩺 Case 5

Six requests for one page

Symptom. The order detail page makes six sequential requests and costs ≈ 8 RU; latency is poor.

Evidence.

  • Model: separate containers for orders, lines, products, customers.
  • Each request is a point read of ≈ 1–2 RU.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Over-normalised — a SQL habit.

Fix. Embed lines (with copied product name and price) and a customer display-name snapshot: one point read ≈ 1–2 RU.

📖 Where to review: page 4.

🩺 Case 6

The post item that keeps growing

Symptom. Writes on popular posts get more expensive every day; one item is approaching 2 MB.

Evidence.

  • The post item contains an embedded comments array.
  • Each new comment rewrites the whole item.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. An unbounded embedded array — cost rises with item size and the 2 MB limit approaches.

Fix. One item per comment with /postId as the key; read a post and its comments with one single-partition query.

📖 Where to review: page 4.

🩺 Case 7

Reads suddenly cost double

Symptom. After a configuration change the read RU bill doubled and cross-region writes became slower.

Evidence.

  • Account default consistency: Strong (was Session).
  • Point reads ≈ 2–4 RU instead of 1–2.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Strong reads use two replicas; strong writes wait for all regions.

Fix. Back to Session; strengthen per request only where a feature needs it.

📖 Where to review: pages 3 and 9.

🩺 Case 8

A big bill, a quiet container

Symptom. A container is provisioned at 20,000 RU/s (manual); the monthly bill is large; usage averages about 9% with an evening peak.

Evidence.

  • Normalized RU: low all day; peak 60% for one hour.
  • No 429s.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Over-provisioned for the peak.

Fix. Autoscale (max sized for the peak; it scales between 10% and 100% of the max) — or serverless for light workloads; re-check utilisation after a week.

📖 Where to review: page 3.

🩺 Case 9

Bonus: a region failed — and the others throttled

Symptom. A region went down; traffic moved to the others and they returned 429s for an hour.

Evidence.

  • Remaining regions provisioned for their own traffic only.
  • Normalized RU high on all partitions in those regions.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. The remaining regions lacked RU for the redirected reads (and writes if promoted).

Fix. Provision (or autoscale) each region for the failover load; rehearse a failover.

📖 Where to review: pages 8 and 11.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

Patterns. Cases 1 and 8 look like “not enough throughput” but are about skew and failover headroom; cases 2 and 4 look alike (“expensive query”) but one is fan-out and the other an index gap — retrieved-vs-output and partitions-queried separate them.

The two bridges. The SQL lab’s method (measure, find the biggest wrong thing, one change) transfers unchanged; only the instruments differ. The bridge page compares them side by side.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Method: 429s or cost? → one partition or all? → retrieved ≈ output? → partitions touched? → item size and indexed paths? → consistency and regions.
  • One change, same request, compare the RU charge.
  • Families: hot key & fan-out (1, 2), index & write cost (3, 4), data shape (5, 6), consistency & provisioning (7, 8), regions (9).
  • Similar symptoms, different causes: check metrics before theorising.
  • Log RequestCharge for your top operations.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"I treat a Cosmos DB cost or throttling problem as an investigation: is it 429s or just cost, is it one partition or all, do retrieved and output documents match, how many partitions does the request touch, how big is the item and how many paths are indexed, and what are the consistency level and region setup. I change one thing and compare the RU charge."
