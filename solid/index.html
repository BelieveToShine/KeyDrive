🧱 SOLID · from “why does every change break something?” to “here is the small, safe refactoring”

Learn the five principles that keep code cheap to change — by refactoring one real class.

One continuous story: why code rots, then S, O, L, I and D one at a time on a single ShopCore OrderService, dependency injection in .NET, a smell-to-refactoring playbook, a full worked refactoring, testability as proof, and the judgement to know when not to apply a principle. Examples are in C#.

🗺️ 12 of 12 concepts are written — the whole journey, from a class that does everything to a design that is easy to change.

🪜 The thinking ladder — used on every page
1 · SMELL

👃 What hurts?

A change that touches many files, a test that needs a database, a class nobody dares edit.

2 · READ

🔬 Who changes this, and why?

List the reasons the class changes and the things it depends on.

3 · FIND

🎯 The principle that is bent

One responsibility too many? A subtype that breaks the contract? A concrete dependency?

4 · GUESS

💡 One refactoring

Extract a class, introduce an interface, invert a dependency.

5 · CHANGE

🔧 One small, safe step

Keep tests green after every step.

6 · PROVE

✅ Re-measure

Fewer files touched per change, tests that run without a database.
