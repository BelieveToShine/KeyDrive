Concept 1 of 12 · 🧱 The problem

Code is judged on the hundredth change, not the first.

The cost of change, cohesion, coupling and reasons to change, and the ShopCore OrderService we will refactor in every page.

❓ "Why do design principles exist, and what do cohesion and coupling mean?"
👁 See it first
🎯 Design principles are not style rules. They exist to keep the cost of the next change low.
📖 Why did this need to exist?

Software is never finished: tax rules change, a new payment provider arrives, marketing wants another promotion. The first version of any program is cheap; the hundredth change is where code is judged. Code that was easy to write but hard to change slowly “rots”: every change takes longer and breaks something unrelated.

Robert C. Martin, who named the SOLID principles, says they expose the dependency-management side of object-oriented design, as opposed to the modeling side, and that poor dependency management leads to code that is hard to change, fragile and non-reusable. In other words: what depends on what decides whether change is cheap.

This page gives you the vocabulary — cohesion, coupling, reason to change — and introduces the practice class we will refactor in every page of this lab.

AbstractionAn interface or base type that hides detailsThe tool that lowers coupling

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

Not a law of nature. “High cohesion, low coupling” has been the guiding idea for decades, though some researchers argue it is an incomplete definition of good design. Treat it as a strong heuristic, not a proof.

SOLID is five heuristics, not five rules. Martin himself says OCP is strategic and no significant program can be 100% closed. Page 11 is about knowing when to stop.

Who coined what. The five principles were collected by Robert C. Martin; page citations link to his own writing. The acronym SOLID itself is a mnemonic added later.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Principles exist to keep the cost of the next change low.
  • Cohesion: how well the parts of a class belong together; coupling: how strongly things depend on each other; aim for high cohesion, low coupling.
  • A “reason to change” is a person or rule that can force an edit.
  • SOLID is about dependency management more than modeling (Martin).
  • Warning signs: shotgun surgery, tests needing infrastructure, fear of editing, big constructors.
  • SOLID is a set of heuristics; judgement still applies.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"Design principles exist to keep the cost of change low. High cohesion means a class has one job, low coupling means classes depend on small abstractions rather than on each other’s details. SOLID gives five concrete heuristics for that, centred on dependency management — and I apply them when I see pain, not by default."
⚠️ Common trap

Treating SOLID as a checklist to satisfy. The goal is cheaper change, not more interfaces.

Refactoring for beauty with no pain. Without a real change pressure you add complexity for nothing (page 11).
