🎯 Before you deploy anything, Azure asks three questions: where does it live, who pays for it, and who may touch it. The hierarchy answers all three.
▶️ Watch it happen: where, who pays, who may touch
📖 Why did this need to exist?

This is where the journey starts, so nothing came before it. Think about what happens without structure: fifty resources dropped into one pile. You cannot tell which ones belong to which app, you cannot send one team its own bill, and you cannot give a contractor access to one app without handing over the keys to everything.

The hierarchy fixes that with four simple layers, each with one job: the tenant says who can sign in, management groups apply rules to many subscriptions at once, a subscription is the unit of billing and access, and a resource group keeps one app's resources together so they can be managed — and deleted — as one.

🧰 The four layers — what each one is for

🏢 Tenant

your organization's directory of identities (Microsoft Entra ID)✅ Pick it when
  • Decide who can sign in: users, groups and apps
  • One directory that many subscriptions trust
  • Every subscription trusts exactly one tenant
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Separating Dev from Prod — use subscriptions for that
  • Thinking of it as a bill — the tenant itself holds identities, not costs

🗂️ Management group

an optional folder that holds subscriptions✅ Pick it when
  • Apply the same role or policy to many subscriptions at once
  • Mirror your company: departments, environments
  • Keep rules in one place instead of copying them
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • A single subscription — you do not need one
  • Holding resources — resources never sit directly in a management group

💳 Subscription

the unit of billing and access, where resources are created✅ Pick it when
  • Give a team or environment its own bill and budget
  • A strong boundary for access and limits
  • Keep Prod and Dev apart
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Grouping the pieces of one app — that is a resource group
  • Creating dozens with no plan — decide a pattern first

📦 Resource group

a container for resources that share a lifecycle✅ Pick it when
  • Keep one app's resources together
  • Delete everything for an app in one step
  • Give a role to just that app
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Grouping by type ("all databases") — it scatters each app
  • Using it as a security wall — permissions come from roles
🧱 Grouping resources: by lifecycle, not by type

🎬 Real-world scenarios — which level?
💰
Finance wants its own bill

The finance team should see and control its own costs.

→ Its own subscription
📜
"Only EU regions" for every team

One rule must apply to every subscription in the company.

→ Policy on a management group
🧹
Delete the demo on Friday

A demo with a web app, a database and storage should vanish in one step.

→ One resource group per demo
🧑‍💻
A contractor sees one app

They must work on the web app and nothing else.

→ A role on that resource group
🚧
Dev must never touch Prod

A mistake in Dev must not be able to reach Prod.

→ Separate subscriptions (plus roles)
🏷️
Cost report by project

Costs must roll up by project across many groups.

→ Tags on resources
▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds) — Azure Resource Manager

Good to know. A subscription trusts one tenant; one tenant can have many subscriptions. Each resource belongs to exactly one resource group (you can move it). ' The location of a resource group is only where its metadata is stored — its resources can live in other regions. Deleting a resource group deletes every resource inside it.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Hierarchy: tenant → management groups (optional) → subscriptions → resource groups → resources.
  • Roles and policies assigned at a level are inherited by everything below it.
  • Subscription = billing and access boundary. Resource group = lifecycle container. Tenant = identity directory.
  • A subscription trusts one tenant; a resource belongs to one resource group; deleting a resource group deletes its resources.
  • Group resources by lifecycle (one app together), not by type.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"Azure organizes everything in a hierarchy: a Microsoft Entra tenant for identity, optional management groups to apply governance across subscriptions, subscriptions as the billing and access boundary, and resource groups to hold resources that share a lifecycle. Roles and policies are inherited downward, so I assign them at the highest sensible level. I group resources by application lifecycle, not by type."
⚠️ Common trap

A resource group is not a region, and not a security wall. Its location only says where its metadata is stored. Resources inside it can be in other regions, and being in the same group gives nobody any permission by itself — permissions come from role assignments.

Second trap: deleting a resource group deletes everything inside it — including the database. Use resource locks and separate groups for anything you cannot afford to lose.

🏢 Tenant

The directory of identities (Microsoft Entra ID). It decides who you are. It is not where resources live.

💳 Subscription

The container where resources are created and billed. It trusts one tenant, and a tenant can have many subscriptions.

▾ 💻 Tiny illustrative example 
# create a resource group — its location says where the group's metadata is stored
az group create --name rg-shop-prod --location westeurope

# delete it — this removes EVERY resource inside the group (the CLI asks you to confirm)
az group delete --name rg-shop-demo

Illustrative shape only — real teams create groups from templates (Bicep) and protect important ones with locks.
