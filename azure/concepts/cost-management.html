📖 Why did this need to exist?

Everything built so far is billed by the hour, by the gigabyte, by the request. Autoscale adds copies, the second region doubles the base, logs grow, a forgotten test environment keeps running. A design can be reliable, secure and fast — and still unaffordable.

The good news is that the cloud also gives you the levers. You can see cost by team and by product, cut what is idle or oversized, and commit to steady use at a lower price. The architect's job is to make cost visible early and to treat it as one more trade-off, not a surprise on the invoice.

🧰 The levers

📊 Cost Management

see, analyse, budget✅ Pick it when
  • Cost analysis by subscription, resource group or tag
  • Budgets with alerts on actual or forecast spend
  • Recommendations and potential savings
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • You expect it to cap spending — a budget alerts, it does not stop things by itself

✂️ Rightsizing and cleanup

stop paying for waste✅ Pick it when
  • Azure Advisor flags VMs with little use over a week and suggests shutting down or a cheaper size
  • Remove orphaned disks, IP addresses and other leftovers
  • Shut down non-production out of hours
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Doing it once — waste comes back

📌 Reservations and savings plans

commit for a lower price✅ Pick it when
  • Steady, predictable use
  • Reservation: prepaid for one or three years for a specific size and region
  • Savings plan for compute: the most flexible commitment, to an hourly spend
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Uncertain or short-lived workloads — a commitment you do not use is waste

🎟️ Spot and Hybrid Benefit

special discounts✅ Pick it when
  • Spot: spare capacity for interruptible work such as batch and dev/test
  • Hybrid Benefit: use existing Windows Server or SQL Server licenses
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Spot for anything that cannot be interrupted — Azure can take the VM back

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds) — details that surprise people

Tags and allocation. Tags are the only way to add your own business context to cost details. Tag inheritance can copy subscription and resource-group tags onto the costs of their resources. Cost allocation rules can split shared costs, but they do not apply to purchases such as reservations and savings plans.

Budgets. They can be scoped to a subscription, a resource group or a set of resources, and can use actual or forecast cost. Because there is no built-in hard stop for a whole subscription, alerts and automation are your main defence.

Spot VMs. Azure can evict a Spot VM when it needs the capacity back, usually giving a short warning through Scheduled Events. Work that is not checkpointed is lost, so they suit interruptible jobs. You choose whether an evicted VM is deallocated (kept) or deleted.

Design choices are cost choices. A second region, extra copies, longer log retention and bigger SKUs all show up on the bill. The Well-Architected cost pillar asks you to weigh them against the value they give — which is the next, last concept.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Cost loop: see (tags, analysis, budgets) → cut waste (idle, orphaned, oversized) → commit (reservations, savings plans, Spot, Hybrid Benefit) → repeat.
  • Reservation = prepaid, one or three years, specific size/region. Savings plan for compute = hourly spend commitment, most flexible. Spot = spare capacity, can be evicted.
  • Budgets alert on actual or forecast spend and can trigger action groups; they do not cap spending by themselves.
  • Tags give cost business meaning; enforce them with Azure Policy; cost allocation does not cover reservations or savings plans.
  • Cut waste before you commit — discounts on waste are still waste.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"I treat cost as a design input and a loop. First visibility: tags enforced by policy, cost analysis and budget alerts. Then waste: idle and oversized resources and orphans, with Advisor's help. Then commitments: reservations for steady fixed use, savings plans for steady flexible spend, Spot for interruptible work and Hybrid Benefit for licenses I already own."
⚠️ Common trap

Buying a reservation before cleaning up. You lock in a discount on resources that should not exist. Remove waste first, then commit to what remains.

Second trap: thinking a budget protects you. A budget sends alerts; by itself it does not stop spending. Attach an action group, and watch the forecast, not only the actual.

📌 Reservation

Commit to a specific resource (size and region) for one or three years, paid up front. Good for fixed, steady use.

🔀 Savings plan

Commit to an hourly spend that applies across eligible compute. More flexible when sizes and services change.
