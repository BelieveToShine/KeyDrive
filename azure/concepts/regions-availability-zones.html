Where you place resources decides how fast and how safe your app is.

A single datacenter is a single point of failure, and distance means delay. Learn what regions and availability zones are, how zonal and zone-redundant resources behave when something breaks, and how to choose a region.

❓ "What is the difference between an Azure region and an availability zone, and when do you use each?"
👁 See it first
🎯 A region is a place. Availability zones are separate groups of datacenters inside it. Spread across zones to survive one failing; use a second region to survive a whole area failing.
▶️ Watch it happen: spread to survive failures
📖 Why did this need to exist?

You now know how to organize resources. But every resource must be created somewhere real — and a single datacenter is a single point of failure. A power cut, a cooling fault or a network problem there takes your whole app down. Far-away users also feel the distance: a request to the other side of the world is slower than one next door.

So Azure gives you regions to put things near your users and under the right laws, and availability zones to survive a datacenter-level failure without leaving the region. Choosing well is one of the cheapest reliability decisions you will ever make.

📖 The words you need
🌍 Region

A place in the world where Azure has datacenters. You choose a region when you create most resources.

🏭 Availability zone

A separate group of datacenters inside a region, with its own power, cooling and networking. Regions that support zones have at least three.

🤝 Paired region

Some regions are paired with another one for disaster-recovery features. Microsoft decides the pairs — you cannot choose your own. Many regions are not paired at all and rely on zones.

📌 Zonal

A resource pinned to one specific zone, like a VM you place in zone 1.

🔀 Zone-redundant

A resource that Azure spreads across zones for you, and fails over if one zone is lost.

🧭 How to choose a region — five questions
1 · Where are my users?

The closer the region, the faster the response. Put the app near the people who use it.

2 · Are there rules about my data?

Laws or company policy may say where data may live. Check this before you build, not after.

3 · Is the service there?

Not every service or feature is available in every region. Check the region list for the services you need.

4 · Does it have zones?

Not every region supports availability zones. If you need zone resilience, pick a region that does.

5 · What does it cost?

Prices can differ between regions, so compare for big workloads.

🎬 Real-world scenarios
🏢
Internal tool, one office

Used 9 to 5 by one team. A short outage is annoying, not costly.

→ One region, one zone is fine
💳
Payments API

Every minute of downtime loses money.

→ Zone-redundant, in a region with zones
🌐
Global SaaS product

Customers on three continents expect fast pages and survive a region outage.

→ Several regions
📜
Data must stay in one country

A law forbids storing customer records abroad.

→ A region inside that country
🧪
Dev and test

Cheap, disposable, nobody is waiting on it.

→ One region, no zone redundancy
💾
Backups

If the primary region is lost, the backups must survive.

→ A copy in another region

🔥 Interview recall
  • Region = a place with datacenters. Availability zone = a separate group of datacenters inside a region (own power, cooling, networking); zone-enabled regions have at least three.
  • Zonal = pinned to one zone (you handle failover). Zone-redundant = spread across zones (Azure handles failover). Prefer zone-redundant.
  • Region pairs are assigned by Microsoft, and many regions are unpaired. Zones protect against a zone failure, not a whole-region failure.
  • Choose a region by: users, data rules, service availability, zone support, price.
  • Zone numbers are logical and mapped per subscription.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"A region is a geographic area with datacenters; availability zones are physically separate groups of datacenters inside a region, each with independent power, cooling and networking. I prefer zone-redundant services so Azure handles failover across zones, use a second region when I must survive a regional outage, and choose the region by user latency, data-residency rules, service availability and zone support."
⚠️ Common trap

Availability zones do not protect you from a whole-region outage. They protect against a zone failing. If losing the entire region would be a disaster, you also need a second region — that is a later concept, but you must design for it from the start.

Second trap: you cannot pick a region pair. Microsoft assigns them, and many regions have no pair — they rely on zones instead. Do not plan around a pairing you assumed.

🏭 Availability zone

A separate group of datacenters inside one region. Protects against a datacenter-level failure, with fast links between zones.

vs.
🌍 Region

A whole geographic area. A second region protects against a regional outage and puts data nearer other users.

▾ 💻 Tiny illustrative examples 
# a zonal VM: pinned to zone 1 (if zone 1 fails, this VM is down)
az vm create --resource-group rg-shop-prod --name vm-web1 --image Ubuntu2204 --zone 1

# a zone-redundant storage account: copies are spread across zones
az storage account create --name stshopprod001 --resource-group rg-shop-prod \
    --location westeurope --sku Standard_ZRS

Illustrative shape only — the storage account name must be globally unique, and VM images and sizes vary by region.
