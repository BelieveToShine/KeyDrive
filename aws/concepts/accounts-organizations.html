Accounts, AWS Organizations, OUs, service control policies and consolidated billing: how to give every workload a safe place to live.

❓ "Why do AWS teams use multiple accounts, and what does AWS Organizations add?"
👁 See it first
🎯 An AWS account is the basic container for everything you build — the wall around your resources, your permissions and your bill — and an organization lets you manage many of those walls together.
📖 Why did this need to exist?

Before you deploy anything on AWS you need three things: a place for it to live, a unit that gets billed, and a boundary that permissions attach to. In AWS all three are the same thing: the account. Nothing comes before it in this journey.

The first account is easy. The trouble comes later, when production, test, a second team and a regulated project all end up in it. One wrong delete or one leaked key then reaches everything. So AWS practice is many accounts — one per environment or workload — tied together by AWS Organizations so they can still be billed, governed and secured as one.

🧰 The pieces

📦 Account

the container and the boundary✅ Pick it when
  • Holds your resources, permissions and usage for one workload or environment
  • A natural unit for billing and for limiting blast radius
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Putting every workload in one account — the boundary then protects nothing

🌳 Organization, root and OUs

manage many accounts as a tree✅ Pick it when
  • A management account, member accounts and organizational units
  • Attach a policy to a unit and it flows to all accounts below
  • A member account belongs to one organization; an account sits in exactly one OU
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Treating OUs as billing groups — they are for applying policy

🛡️ Service control policies (SCPs)

guardrails for whole accounts✅ Pick it when
  • Limit what accounts may do, whatever their own IAM says
  • Block regions or services you never use
  • They filter — they never grant access
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Expecting an SCP to give someone permission — IAM (concept 3) does that

💳 Consolidated billing

one bill for the whole organization✅ Pick it when
  • The management account pays for all member accounts
  • Combined usage can share volume pricing discounts, Reserved Instance discounts and Savings Plans
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Consolidated billing alone gives no policies — the "all features" mode does (and is the default)

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds) — who does what in an organization

Management account. The account that created the organization; this cannot be changed later. It can create accounts, invite others, attach policies and pays the charges for the whole organization. AWS recommends keeping workloads out of it, because SCPs and similar policies do not apply to the management account.

Member accounts are every other account. An account can belong to only one organization at a time.

SCPs are filters. An SCP never grants permissions; it limits what permissions can be used. A principal's effective permissions are the overlap between the SCPs and its own IAM policies.

Feature sets. "All features" (the default) turns on policies and service integrations. "Consolidated billing" alone covers only billing.

Landing zones. Setting up the standard set of accounts and guardrails once, so every new account starts safe, is the idea behind a landing zone — the last concept in this journey.

🔥 Interview recall
  • An account is the container, the permission boundary and the billing unit. Use several to limit blast radius.
  • Organizations: a management account, member accounts, OUs in a tree. Policies attached to a node flow down.
  • SCPs filter what accounts may use; they never grant access. Effective permission = SCP ∩ IAM.
  • Consolidated billing: one bill, shared volume and commitment discounts. "All features" is the default and adds policies.
  • Keep workloads out of the management account.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"In AWS the account is the basic boundary for resources, permissions and billing, so I use separate accounts for production and non-production and for teams with different needs. AWS Organizations ties them into a tree of OUs under a management account, gives one consolidated bill and lets me attach service control policies, which only limit — never grant — what the accounts can do. I keep workloads out of the management account."
⚠️ Common trap

One big account "for now". It is the easiest start and the hardest thing to untangle later. Create the accounts you need early; they cost nothing by themselves.

Second trap: thinking an SCP gives access. It does not. If IAM does not allow an action, the SCP cannot make it allowed — it can only take permission away.
