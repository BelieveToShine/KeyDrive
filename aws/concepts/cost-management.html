📖 Why did this need to exist?

Everything built so far is billed by the hour, by the gigabyte, by the request. Auto Scaling adds copies, the second Region doubles the base, logs grow, a forgotten test environment keeps running. A design can be reliable, secure and fast — and still unaffordable.

The good news is that AWS also gives you the levers. You can see cost by team and by workload, cut what is idle or oversized, and commit to steady use at a lower price. The architect's job is to make cost visible early and to treat it as one more trade-off, not a surprise on the invoice.

🧰 The levers

📊 Cost Explorer, Budgets, Cost Categories

see, analyse, budget✅ Pick it when
  • Cost Explorer shows which services drive spend; cost allocation tags break it down by team or workload
  • AWS Budgets: custom cost and usage budgets with alerts; filter by tags
  • Cost Categories group accounts and tags into business views
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Expecting a budget to stop spending — it alerts

📏 Compute Optimizer and cleanup

stop paying for waste✅ Pick it when
  • Recommends right-sized compute for your workload
  • Start with a generic size, then resize on real CPU and memory use
  • Remove resources nobody uses
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Doing it once — waste comes back

📌 Savings Plans and Reserved Instances

commit for a lower price✅ Pick it when
  • Savings Plans: a commitment measured in dollars per hour for one or three years, more flexible
  • Reserved Instances: can be cheaper still for steady workloads, less flexible
  • Cost Explorer makes purchase recommendations
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Uncertain or short-lived workloads — an unused commitment is waste

🎟️ Spot Instances

spare capacity at a discount✅ Pick it when
  • Request spare compute at a discounted hourly rate, with no upfront commitment
  • Suits non-critical, interruptible work
⛔ Look elsewhere when
  • Anything that cannot be interrupted

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds) — details that surprise people

Commitments. Savings Plans offer lower prices than On-Demand in exchange for a specific usage commitment, measured in dollars per hour, for a one- or three-year period. Reserved Instances can be cheaper still for steady workloads but are less flexible.

Spot. Spot Instances let you request spare compute capacity at a discounted hourly rate with no upfront commitment. AWS guidance is to use them for non-critical workloads.

Tags. Cost allocation tags combined with Cost Explorer show which teams or parts of the organization drive spend; Cost Categories group accounts and tags into business-unit views that also appear in Budgets and Cost Anomaly Detection.

Design choices are cost choices. A second Region, extra copies, longer log retention and bigger instances all show up on the bill. The Well-Architected cost pillar asks you to weigh them against the value they give — which is the next, last concept.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Cost loop: see (tags, Cost Explorer, budgets) → cut waste (idle, oversized) → commit (Savings Plans, Reserved Instances, Spot) → repeat.
  • Savings Plans = commitment in dollars per hour, one or three years, more flexible. Reserved Instances = less flexible. Spot = spare capacity, can be interrupted.
  • AWS Budgets alert on cost and usage; they do not cap spending by themselves.
  • Cost allocation tags give cost business meaning; commitment fees cannot be tagged in advance — use amortized cost.
  • Resize with Compute Optimizer before you commit; cut waste before discounts.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"I treat cost as a design input and a loop. First visibility: cost allocation tags, Cost Explorer and budget alerts. Then waste: idle and oversized resources, checked with Compute Optimizer. Then commitments: Savings Plans for steady flexible spend, Reserved Instances for steady fixed use, and Spot for interruptible work."
⚠️ Common trap

Buying a commitment before cleaning up. You lock in a discount on resources that should not exist. Remove waste first, then commit to what remains.

Second trap: thinking a budget protects you. A budget sends alerts; by itself it does not stop spending. Watch forecasts as well as actuals, and act on the alert.
