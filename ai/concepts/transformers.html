Concept 5 of 17 · 🎓 Foundations

Transformers let every word look at every other word directly, instead of waiting in line.

Neural networks gave us the building block. Transformers are the specific network shape that turned out to handle sequences — sentences, code, anything ordered — dramatically better than what came before.

❓ "What problem does 'attention' actually solve?"
👁 See it first
🎯 Transformers = a network built around attention, so every position can look at every other position directly.
📖 Why did this need to exist?

Earlier sequence models processed one word at a time, in order, carrying a running "memory" of everything seen so far forward to the next step. That works, but two things suffer: information from early words has to survive many hops to influence a much later word, weakening long-range relationships; and because each step depends on the previous one finishing, the whole sequence can't be processed in parallel.

The transformer's answer is attention: instead of relaying information hop by hop, every position computes, in one step, how much it should weigh every other position — near or far, it doesn't matter. That removes the long-range weakening problem and lets the whole computation run in parallel, which is a large part of why transformers scale so well.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

"Attention" is a weighting mechanism: for a given word, it computes a relevance score against every other word in the sequence, turns those scores into weights, and blends the other words' information according to those weights. A word usually ends up paying most attention to a handful of genuinely relevant words, not equally to all of them — the mechanism can reach any position, but it still has to learn which ones matter.

This "attend to everything, weigh what matters" building block, stacked in layers like any other neural network, is what almost every modern large language model is built from.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Attention lets every position connect directly to every other position, not just neighbors.
  • It solves two problems at once: long-range relationships weakening, and step-by-step processing blocking parallelism.
  • Attention computes relevance weights, then blends other positions' information by those weights.
  • Transformers = neural networks built around stacked attention layers.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"Transformers replace step-by-step sequence processing with attention, where every position computes a relevance-weighted view of every other position in one step. That fixes the long-range-dependency weakness of earlier sequential models and lets the computation run in parallel, which is a big part of why transformers scale so well."
⚠️ Common trap

Attention doesn't mean "reads the whole input equally." Every position can reach every other position directly, but the learned weights usually concentrate on a few genuinely relevant ones. "Direct access" and "pays attention to everything the same amount" are not the same claim — only the first one is true.

▾ 💻 Tiny illustrative example 
# Attention, at the shape level (not the real math)
words = ["The", "trophy", "didn't", "fit"]

# for one word, compute a relevance score against every word
scores  = [relevance(words[3], w) for w in words]   # incl. itself
weights = softmax(scores)                          # turn scores into weights that sum to 1
context = sum(w * embed(word) for w, word in zip(weights, words))

Illustrative shape only — real attention runs this for every word, across many layers, with learned projections rather than a hand-written relevance().
