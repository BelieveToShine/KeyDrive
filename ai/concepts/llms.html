Concept 6 of 19 · 🎓 Foundations

An LLM is typically a transformer, trained at massive scale — scale is the big lever, not a new mechanism.

Transformers gave us attention. Modern LLMs are transformer-based language models that take that design and train it much bigger, on much more text, until its capability broadens dramatically — real models still vary in architectural details, but scale is the core story.

❓ "What actually makes something an LLM instead of just 'a transformer'?"
👁 See it first
🎯 LLM = a transformer-based language model, trained at large scale in size and training data, until capability broadens dramatically.
📖 Why did this need to exist?

A transformer at small scale already works — but its capability stays narrow, good mainly at whatever specific task it was trained for. Scaling a transformer-style architecture up — many more layers and parameters, vastly more training text — is what produces a model broadly capable across language tasks it was never specifically trained for.

This "scale unlocks broader capability" pattern is one of the most well-established results in the field, even though the exact capability a given increase in scale will unlock isn't fully predictable in advance. "LLM" isn't one exact architecture — it's the name for a transformer-based language model once it's been trained at this scale; real models vary in specific architectural choices (encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only, mixture-of-experts, and others), but they're all telling the same scaling story.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

"Large" describes two things at once: the model's own size (how many layers and parameters it has) and the size of what it was trained on (how much text). Both matter — a huge model trained on very little text, or a small model drowned in data, doesn't get you the same result as scaling both together.

Everything downstream from here — prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, agents — is about how to actually use a model at this scale, not about redesigning its architecture from scratch.

🔥 Interview recall
  • LLM = typically a transformer-based language model, trained at large scale — not one fixed, exact architecture.
  • Scaling a transformer-style design up is what produces broad, general-purpose language capability.
  • "Large" covers both model size (layers/parameters) and training data size — both matter.
  • Capability gains from scale are well-established as a pattern, not perfectly predictable case by case.
  • Real LLMs still vary in architectural details (encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only, mixture-of-experts, etc.) — scale is the shared story, not a claim that every LLM is identical under the hood.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"An LLM is typically a transformer-based language model at a much larger scale — more layers and parameters, trained on far more text. It's fundamentally the same transformer-style architecture as a small transformer, though real models vary in specific details; scale is what turns a narrow skill into broad, general-purpose language capability."
⚠️ Common trap

"LLM" names a scale, not a new mechanism. Don't describe LLMs as if they use a fundamentally different technique from transformers — they're still fundamentally attention-based, even though real models vary in architectural details (encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only, mixture-of-experts, and more). If asked "what's the difference between a transformer and an LLM," the honest answer is mainly scale, not a wholesale change in technique.
