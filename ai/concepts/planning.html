Concept 13 of 17 · 🤖 Acting on goals

Planning breaks a goal into ordered steps up front, instead of improvising one at a time.

The agent loop decides its very next step each time through. Planning is what lays out the whole sequence of steps a complex goal actually needs, before running any of them.

❓ "Isn't planning just the 'Plan' step of the agent loop? What's different?"
👁 See it first
🎯 Planning = breaking a complex goal into an ordered sequence of sub-steps before executing any of them.
📖 Why did this need to exist?

A complex goal — like planning a multi-day trip — often can't be handled well by deciding only the very next step at a time. Some steps depend on others happening first, and a purely reactive, one-step-at-a-time approach can miss that structure entirely.

Planning breaks the goal into an ordered sequence of sub-steps up front, giving the agent a coherent path to follow — even though each individual step still runs through the normal plan-act-observe loop.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

Planning up front doesn't mean the plan is rigid. If an early step's real result changes what's needed later — a flight turns out to be sold out — that observation can feed back into revising the remaining plan, not just the next action. "Planned" means "has an explicit ordered structure to start from," not "unchangeable."

For a simple, single-step goal, there's nothing to plan — planning earns its keep specifically once a goal has multiple, order-dependent parts.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Planning decomposes a complex goal into an ordered sequence of sub-steps, up front.
  • It exists because some steps depend on others happening first — reacting one step at a time can miss that.
  • A plan can still be revised mid-execution based on real results.
  • Simple, single-step goals don't need planning — it earns its keep on multi-part goals.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"Planning breaks a complex goal into an ordered sequence of sub-steps before execution starts, so steps that depend on each other happen in the right order. Each step still runs through the normal act-and-observe loop, and the plan itself can be revised if a step's real result changes what's needed later."
⚠️ Common trap

Planning and the agent loop's "Plan" step aren't quite the same thing. The loop's "Plan" decides the very next action, every iteration. Planning, in this broader sense, is about laying out the whole ordered sequence for a complex goal up front. The loop's Plan step can lean on that broader plan — they're related, not identical.

🧭 "Plan" (loop step)

Decides the next single action, every iteration.

vs.
🗺️ Planning (this concept)

Decomposes the whole goal into ordered steps, up front.

▾ 💻 Tiny illustrative example 
# Decompose once, then run each step through the normal agent loop
steps = decompose(goal)  # e.g. ["book flights", "book hotel", "build itinerary"]

for step in steps:
    result = agent_loop(step)   # each step still: plan, act, observe
    if result.changes_remaining_plan:
        steps = replan(steps, result)  # adjust what's left, if needed

Illustrative shape only — real planning systems handle dependencies and replanning with more structure than this.
