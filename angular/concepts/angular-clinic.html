📖 Why did this need to exist?

Real tickets say “the checkout feels slow” or “the page went blank”, not “state too high”. The cases start the way tickets do, and all of them use the same ShopUI app and the same illustrative numbers as the lab.

Every case is solvable with the ladder: measure, read the profile, find the biggest wrong thing, one hypothesis, one change, prove it.

🧭 The ladder, Angular edition
1 · MEASURE ★

📏 How slow, exactly?

Change-detection cycles and milliseconds in Angular DevTools — a number, not a feeling.

2 · READ ★

🔬 What was checked, and why?

Read the profiler: which components ran and what triggered them.

3 · FIND ★

🎯 The biggest wrong thing

A default-strategy tree, a function call in a template, a leaked subscription, a heavy bundle?

4 · GUESS ★

💡 One hypothesis

Missing OnPush? No track? Hot observable? Eager route?

5 · CHANGE ★

🔧 Change one thing

Never two at once.

6 · PROVE ★

✅ Re-measure

Same interaction, fewer checks, fewer milliseconds.

Ask in this order: wrong or slow? → how many components were checked, and why? → what changed in their inputs or signals? → does the cost come from checking, from subscriptions, from loading, or from shipping code? → what is the smallest change that tests my guess?

🩺 Case 1

Typing in the search box lags

Symptom. Each keystroke takes ≈ 40 ms and the page stutters; only the list changes.

Evidence.

  • Profiler: one cycle checks 504 components; ProductCards ≈ 35 ms of 40.
  • ProductCard uses the default (Eager) strategy; its inputs never change.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Eager components are re-checked on every pass even when their inputs are unchanged.

Fix. ChangeDetectionStrategy.OnPush on ProductCard. Re-record: 4 checked, ≈ 3 ms.

📖 Where to review: pages 2 and 3.

🩺 Case 2

The list flashes on every refetch

Symptom. After each refetch the 500 cards are rebuilt and typed notes are lost.

Evidence.

  • DevTools: 980 DOM operations per refetch (500 destroyed, 480 created).
  • Code: @for (p of list(); track p).

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Tracking by object identity: new objects after a refetch look like new items.

Fix. track p.id. Re-record: 20 DOM operations.

📖 Where to review: page 4.

🩺 Case 3

A price method logs hundreds of times

Symptom. Support notices a template method running on every keystroke.

Evidence.

  • console.log in formatPrice(p): 500 lines per keystroke.
  • The card template calls formatPrice(product()).

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. A method in a binding runs on every check of that component.

Fix. The currency pipe or a computed(). Zero runs per keystroke.

📖 Where to review: page 4.

🩺 Case 4

Memory grows each time the cart page is opened

Symptom. After ten visits the tab is slow and the heap has ten copies of CartPage.

Evidence.

  • Chrome heap snapshot: ten live CartPage instances.
  • Code: ngOnInit() { this.cart$.subscribe(…) } with no cleanup.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. A subscription outlives the component.

Fix. The async pipe, toSignal or takeUntilDestroyed(). One instance after ten visits.

📖 Where to review: page 6.

🩺 Case 5

An OnPush list never updates

Symptom. After adding OnPush to the list, “Add to cart” stops updating the cart view.

Evidence.

  • The service does this.items.push(item) on a plain array field.
  • The Components tab shows the array reference is unchanged.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. OnPush compares references; a mutated array looks unchanged and nothing notifies Angular.

Fix. A signal with an immutable update: items.update(l => [...l, item]).

📖 Where to review: page 3.

🩺 Case 6

Search sends a request per keystroke

Symptom. Typing “shi” sends three requests and sometimes shows results for “sh”.

Evidence.

  • Network panel: three requests; the second answer arrives last.
  • Code: mergeMap(q => http.get(…)) with no debounce.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. mergeMap runs all requests and lets answers arrive out of order.

Fix. debounceTime + distinctUntilChanged + switchMap. One request, newest answer.

📖 Where to review: page 6.

🩺 Case 7

Slow first load on phones

Symptom. The home page takes seconds to respond on a mid-range phone; change detection is fast.

Evidence.

  • Build output: initial JavaScript 420 KB; checkout 90 KB and admin 150 KB are inside it.
  • Routes use component: for every page.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Download and parse cost: rarely-visited routes ship in the first bundle.

Fix. loadComponent / loadChildren, and @defer for below-the-fold blocks. Initial JavaScript ≈ 180 KB.

📖 Where to review: pages 9 and 10.

🩺 Case 8

The checkout form updates the model but not the screen

Symptom. After migrating to zoneless, patching the form from a service no longer updates the view.

Evidence.

  • Zoneless is the default in the new app.
  • The service calls form.patchValue(…) from a timer.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. Reactive-form model updates do not schedule change detection by themselves.

Fix. Read the form state through a signal or toSignal, or call markForCheck().

📖 Where to review: pages 2 and 8.

🩺 Case 9

Bonus: hydration error after adding a widget

Symptom. After adding a third-party chart, the console shows NG0500 and the page flashes on load.

Evidence.

  • SSR is enabled; the widget writes elements into the DOM with native APIs.
  • The client DOM differs from the server DOM.

Your move: which family is it, and what is your one change?

▾ Reveal the diagnosis

Diagnosis. DOM changed outside Angular, so hydration cannot match.

Fix. Create the widget in afterNextRender (browser only), or as a last resort ngSkipHydration on that component.

📖 Where to review: page 10, lifecycle dive.

▾ Go one level deeper (60 seconds)

Patterns. Cases 1, 3 and 5 all look like “Angular is slow” but each has a different lever: OnPush, a template method, a mutated array. The profile and the code tell you which — never the symptom alone.

Case 7 is not a change-detection problem. The profiler showed fast cycles; only the build output exposed the cost. Match the tool to the layer (check, load, ship).

Make your own clinic. Take five real tickets, strip the diagnosis, keep symptom and evidence, and run it with your team.

🔥 Interview recall
  • Method: wrong or slow? → how many components checked and why? → what changed? → check, subscribe, load or ship? → smallest test of the guess.
  • One change, same interaction, same build, compare the profile.
  • Families: checks & state placement (1, 3, 5), identity & subscriptions (2, 4, 8), loading & shipping (6, 7), resilience (9).
  • Similar symptoms, different causes: read the profile before theorising.
  • Measure the layer you suspect: DevTools profiler for checks, heap snapshot for leaks, build output for shipping.
🎤 Say this in an interview
"I treat an Angular problem as an investigation: is it wrong or slow, how many components were checked and why, what changed in their inputs or signals, and is the cost in checking, subscriptions, loading or shipped code. I form one hypothesis, change one thing, and re-measure the same interaction in the same build."
⚠️ Common trap

Adding OnPush everywhere first. Several cases had other causes: track, a template method, a subscription, a mutated array.

Skipping the evidence. In every case one number — components checked, KB, or a line in the code — decided the diagnosis.
